## 0. How we work this week

### Roles

| model | does | does not |
|---|---|---|
| **Fable** | critical changes: anything every bot runs every tick (the auction `BotWill`, reflexes, combat, companies, navigation), money flows, persistence/serialization, population invariants; **every review** | bounded content work Opus can do |
| **Opus** | ordinary changes: one subsystem, a known cause, a mechanic inside an existing module, institutions, PLAN-life items after Fable's design note; tooling | reviewing its own work |
| **Sonnet** | log analysis before a change (what fails, how often, where), and **checking every fix after deploy** (before/after numbers on the same measure) | editing code or configs |

**Reviews.** Patrick's rule of 07.10 (two thorough reviews with arguments and assumptions per change) stands, with
Fable as the reviewer. Proposal to save the weekly limit: **S-size changes get one Fable review, M and above get two
independent ones.** Findings are fixed or rejected with a reason in the DECISIONS entry.

**At most three agents at once** (`night-quota-burnout`): one Fable implementer, one Opus implementer, one Sonnet
analyst is the default shape of a wave; reviews run as the implementers finish.

### Standing rules every agent gets in its brief

- **Staging lives on `O:`**, never in the session scratchpad: `O:\ModernUOfork\work\<agent>` (or a `git worktree`,
  see D4), deleted after the merge. A copy of the tree in `%TEMP%` costs Patrick's Windows logon (131 copies =
  1.16 M files, 255 s logon; `no-repo-copies-in-temp`). The scratchpad holds patches, reports and logs only.
- `merge3.py` only with **full** copies; never trim its output; `deleted > 0` that nobody intended = stop
  (`merge3-partial-tree-deletes`).
- Compile check while the shard runs: `dotnet build Projects/BotAIv2/mindedBots/BotMindAI.csproj -c Release
  "-p:OutDir=<scratch>\build\"` — it builds BotAIv2 and BotMindAI and prints an honest error count.
- Deploy only with `handover-0110/tools/deploy.sh` (save through Argus's door → stop → build → `START_SHARD.bat`).
  Never stop the shard inside an autosave; never delete the world save; a wipe only on Patrick's word (`do reset`
  must clear every new store).
- Configs override code and their keys are PascalCase; a dial changed live through `do dial` must also be written to
  the config or the next boot undoes it.
- Offices exactly one each (Captain, Baron, Architect, Sage) at any population.
- Honest money: no gold from nothing, no handouts — **poor bots hunt**. Honest counting: a new gate gets a new
  counter; never improve a share by changing what counts.
- A bot sees with its own eyes: danger = zones (prior) + creatures in sight (overrides locally).
- English in code, comments and docs; one `DECISIONS.md` §4.1 entry per build; `MAP.md` regenerated with
  `regen-map.py` after a wave.
- **KPI guard around every deploy:** paths, work (panel share without deaths, and with deaths), Needs (first hour),
  deaths per bot-hour, median wealth and the poor hunters' share (`The island:`), traders, the loop (ms a look). A build that drops any of them is fixed or rolled
  back before the next wave.

### Sizes and approaches used below

Size: **S** — a dial or a local fix, under an hour, one review. **M** — one subsystem, a known cause. **L** — several
files or a new mechanic in an existing system. **XL** — a new subsystem or a change every bot feels.

Approach: **decision** (Patrick's), **dial** (config / live through the door), **measure-first** (cause not known —
Sonnet first), **fix** (cause known), **mechanic** (new behaviour inside an existing module), **subsystem** (new module),
**infra** (tools and process).

**Order:** A1 → A2 → A4 → A5 → A5' → A3 → A6 → A7 → A8 (A1 and A2 change what every bot does every hour, so they go

### 5.1 Part A — the approved core

#### A1. The daily cycle — **Fable**, XL, subsystem
- **Seen:** the island has a rhythm. Markets fill at dawn; bots work through the day; some eat at noon; in the evening
  taverns and firesides are full and the roads empty; at night the watch walks, thieves come out, most bots sleep at
  home, at an inn or by a fire. A player at 22:00 finds a different island from one at 10:00.
- **Parts:**
  - a shard clock of named parts of the day on real local time (like `BotPrime`): dawn, morning, noon, afternoon,
    evening, night;
  - a timetable per bot drawn at birth from class and nature (`BotTraits`: homebody/wanderer, sociable/solitary…) —
    wake hour, meal hours, evening habit (tavern, fireside, home, prowl), sleep place; night owls exist (thieves,
    watch, some hunters);
  - the timetable is a **weight on offers, not a wall**: evening raises tavern/fireside/stories/home and lowers long
    journeys; a starving or broke bot still works at night (a factor without a floor is a veto);
  - sleep: `BotRest` becomes going to bed at its own home (A4) or an inn, with the rested buff; prime time's offline
    share is filled first by bots whose bedtime it is;
  - a festivals calendar every few days, announced a day ahead (harvest fair, night of lanterns, midsummer) on
    `BotEvents` fairs and taverns, with contests (B5) on festival days;
  - weather per region a few hours long: storms hold ships and thin the market, clear days raise hunting and caravans.
- **Builds on:** `BotPrime`, `BotRest`, `BotInns`, `BotTaverns`/`BotSupper`, `BotEvents`/`BotFair`, `BotTraits`, the
  offer weighting in `BotWill`.
- **Why Fable:** it weighs every offer of every bot every hour — the auction is the most sensitive code on the shard.
  The festivals and weather pieces can go to Opus once the core weight is in.
- **Measure:** offers taken by part of day (a table in `/api/state`), tavern guests by hour, sleepers at home / inn /
  field; work and Needs must not fall.

#### A2. Dreams — one life goal per bot — Opus, Fable review, L, mechanic; after A1
- **Seen:** every bot has a dream on the dashboard ("Nessa dreams of a house by the sea", "Varen wants to lead The
  Wolf", "Brokk will be a grandmaster smith", "Thalia swore revenge on the ogre that killed her friend"), its progress,
  and the story of how it came true or was given up.
- **Parts:** dream kinds with a test of progress and of fulfilment from real state — own a house (A4), a bigger house,
  grandmaster in the class's main skill, lead or found a guild, an office in a guild, win the championship, take a
  relic, see every town, kill a named creature (A8), revenge (`BotGrudge`), marry (A7), teach an apprentice to
  journeyman (A5), save N gold, furnish a house fully. Chosen at birth and after each fulfilment from traits, class and
  life (a greedy bot dreams of gold, a vindictive one of revenge, a sociable one of a family); one at a time, a small
  chance to give it up after long failure. A dream is a bounded weight ×1.1–1.3 on offers that move it — never a veto,
  never a payment. Fulfilment: a biography milestone, a line said in town, a tavern celebration, a new dream.
- **Builds on:** `BotBiography`/`BotLife`, `BotTraits`, `BotGrudge`, `BotProgress` (titles), `BotAbode`, officers,
  tourney, relics.
- **Measure:** dreams by kind, fulfilled per day, given up, mean days to fulfil; shares must not fall.

#### A4. Houses that are homes — furnishing — Opus, Fable review, L, mechanic; after A2
- **Seen:** a bot who bought a house (`BotAbode`) furnishes it over time — bed, table and chairs, a bookshelf with its
  diary (A5'), a chest, rugs, a forge or loom for crafters, trophies (a relic it won, a head from a legend hunt), a sign
  with its name. Friends come for a house-warming. A player can walk into a lived-in house.
- **Parts:** a furnishing plan per house size and class (engine furniture placed by the house's own rules, locked
  down); bought honestly — furniture is a want on the Needs board / an order to carpenters, tailors (rugs, curtains),
  smiths: real demand for crafts that have little today, paid from the purse above its keep, slowly; use — sleep at
  home (A1), surplus into the house chest (`BotSurplus`), house-warmings and evening visits for sociable bots; upgrade —
  a dream (A2) of a bigger house, the old one sold honestly.
- **Builds on:** `BotAbode`, `BotEstate`/`BotFittings` (guild halls are already fitted), `BotMarket`/`BotLines`,
  carpenters, `BotSurplus`.
- **Fable reviews** house placement and the money flow.
- **Measure:** houses furnished %, furniture bought (gp, items), carpenter work taken/finished, visits; no minting.

#### A5. Master and apprentice — Opus, Fable review, L, mechanic; after D3
- **Seen:** a grandmaster smith walks with an apprentice who fetches ore, works the bellows, crafts beside the master
  and grows faster; later the apprentice opens a stall and takes its own apprentice. Mages learn Inscription from the

- **Builds on:** `BotTutor` (the Captain and the Sage teach already), crafts, `BotScribe`, `BotBiography`.
- **Measure:** pairs, graduations, circle-6/8 scribes over days, the Needs scroll share. Population to 400 waits for

#### A6. Telling stories — Opus, M, mechanic; after A1 and A3

#### B4. Courts and trials
A caught thief or murderer is tried in the town hall: witnesses (bots who saw it) speak, the steward or a judge
decides — a fine to the victim, the cells, exile; the trial is spoken aloud and logged. Builds on `BotCrime`, stewards,
`BotWitness`. Measure: trials, verdicts by kind, fines paid to victims.

#### B5. Games and contests
Dice and chess in taverns (small honest stakes between bots); on festival days an archery contest, a foot race, a
fishing contest, a cooking contest, with prizes from the town purse. Measure: games played, gold changing hands,
contests held, entrants.

#### B3. Letters
Friends and kin in other towns write letters (a real in-game letter item); couriers carry them for a small fee; a
letter can carry news, an invitation to a wedding, a request for help, or gold (honest, from the sender's purse). Makes
the roads social and gives couriers work. Measure: letters sent, delivered, lost, fees paid.

#### B6. Sickness and healers
Occasional illness (a fever after a swamp, a plague in one town) lowers stamina and work; healers and herbalists cure
it for a fee; a plague brings quarantine and Needs for cures — real work for Healers beyond combat (infirmaries do
almost nothing today). Fable reviews, because it lowers work on purpose and must not read as a defect in the shares.
Measure: cases, cures, fees, the share of work lost to illness reported apart.

#### B2. Pilgrimages and virtues
Bots walk to the eight shrines after a death in the family, before a war, or as a dream; virtue standing (the engine's
virtues where they exist) colours speech and choices; a pilgrimage is a long journey with companions. Builds on travel,
gates, companies. Measure: pilgrimages begun, finished, deaths on the way.

#### B1. Bards and songs
A bard (a class leaning or a Musicianship hobby) composes short songs from the biographies and the Chronicle and
performs them in taverns and at festivals; a song raises the hero's fame and the tavern's guests; players hear them.
Builds on A3/A6, `BotTaverns`, the engine's Musicianship. Measure: songs made, performances, guests drawn.

#### B10. The island gazette
A daily "Britannia Herald" from the Chronicle (A3), market prices, wars, championship results, births and weddings,
the legend's sightings — a dashboard page and an in-game book on every bank's counter, read aloud by heralds
(officers). Measure: issues, readers (book opened), herald readings.

#### B7. Collectors and museums
Some bots collect (rare dyes, paintings, gems, books, trophies); collectors trade among themselves and buy from
adventurers; a guild hall can hold a museum room that raises the guild's appeal. Measure: collections, trades between
collectors, museum rooms and the appeal they add.

#### B8. The graveyard
Bots deleted at a wipe or who died many times get a tombstone with an epitaph from their story; friends visit on
festival days; the Chronicle lists the fallen of the week. (Bots do not die for good; tombstones mark heroic deaths and
the old world's dead after a wipe.) Measure: tombstones, visits.

#### B9. Bots and players — **Fable**
Bots remember the players they meet (Patrick): a reputation per player (helped, robbed, fought beside); bots post
requests a player can answer on a board in town (fetch, escort, kill), pay from their own purse, and tell stories about
the player. The island reacts to the human living in it. Fable, because it is the first thing on the shard that reads a
real client's actions and must never let a player's presence break the bots' own rules (the world sleeps without a
client; bots have no NetState). Measure: players remembered, requests posted and answered, gold paid.
