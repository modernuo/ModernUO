# Measuring bot strength on the proving ground

This folder is one topic of the research notes on autonomous bots for a ModernUO shard (Ultima Online, Renaissance
era). It covers the night and day of 26.09.2026, builds 234 to 264, when the shard started to measure a bot's
strength by making a copy of the bot fight a real creature, instead of computing it from a formula.

Everything below comes from the shard's logs, the decision log (`Projects/BotAIv2/DECISIONS.md`, section 4.1) and
the code in `Projects/BotAIv2/BotProving/`. The logs give 1,248 single fights and 115 company fights between 01:36
and 15:28. Every number that comes from a log line is regenerated by `extract.py`. Times are the shard's local clock
as the logs print it.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `README.md` | This write-up. |
| `index.html` | Charts of the same data. Open it in a browser; it needs no server and makes no requests. |
| `extract.py` | Rebuilds everything in `data/` from a logs folder (Python 3, standard library only). |
| `data/` | The measurements as CSV, plus `summary.json` with every aggregate quoted here. |

## 1. The question

Until build 234 every judgement of a bot's strength rested on one number, `BotThreat.Power`
(`Projects/BotAIv2/BotCombat/BotThreat.cs`):

    Power = max(1, HitsMax) × max(1, average hit + Magery / 2)

The average hit is `(MinDamage + MaxDamage) / 2` of the weapon in hand (of the creature's own damage for a creature).
Magery / 2 is added for every body, so a caster's number is mostly its Magery. The same formula, applied to one freshly
built creature of each kind, is that creature's "might", and each dungeon is ranked by the might of its worst
creature. The delve sent a band into a dungeon when its four best `Power` values added up to 1.5 times the might of
the dungeon's worst creature.

Nobody had checked the formula against a fight. It cannot see armour, hit chance, swing speed, Tactics and Anatomy
bonuses, bandages, bottles, healing spells, poison, magic resistance, or a weapon's magic. On 26.09.2026 the shard
owner asked for the bots to be measured by a fight with their real kit.

## 2. The method

### The double

`BotStandIn` (`BotStandIn.cs`) is a copy of one bot for one fight. It is a `PlayerMobile`, not a `BotMobile`, so no
population code (danger map, wars, the bots' records) ever sees it. It gets the bot's body, raw stats, every skill's
base and cap, a copy of everything worn, and from the pack what a bot uses in a fight: bandages, potions, reagents,
scrolls, spellbook, arrows and bolts, spare weapons (the whole pack from build 238). Items are copied with the
engine's own dupe, so a weapon's magic stays. The double and the creature are deleted when the fight ends; nothing
leaves the ring.

### The fight

`BotTrial` (`BotTrial.cs`) puts the double and one creature three tiles either side of a ring's middle in Green Acres
(rings at 5510/5550, 1150/1190; two rings for single fights, one for companies). A referee beat runs every 200 ms.
The engine swings the weapons. The double acts with the bot's own code (`BotDriver.cs`): attack spells, a bottle when
low or poisoned, a healing spell or a bandage when hurt, blade or bow drawn as the bot would, one step per beat to
close the distance. It never flees. The fight ends when one side dies, at 180 s ("called at the cap"), or after 45 s
in which nobody was hurt.

### The reading

    R = (share of the creature killed) × HitsMax / max(damage taken − health healed, 2% of HitsMax)

R is how many of these creatures, one after another, the bot's health would pay for. R is capped at 5 (at 20 in build
234). For a class whose role is Caster, R is also limited by mana: share killed × mana pool / mana spent, when more
than a tenth of the pool was spent. For a lost fight R is close to the share of the creature killed. A fight's
proven strength is R × the creature's might, on the same scale the dungeons are ranked by. R ≥ 1 counts as beating
the creature.

### The ladder

The rungs are each dungeon's worst creature, weakest first, read off the spawners at every boot: orcish mage (the Orc
Caves), juka mage (Wrong), ogre lord (Despise), then lich lord, poison elemental, dragon, white wyrm, balron, ancient
lich, ancient wyrm (the order of the middle rungs changes between boots). A bot is tried on the lowest rung it has not
beaten, climbs one rung per win, and is tried again when its build changes or after 90 minutes. Two things make the
scale noisy: might is `Power` of one freshly rolled creature per boot (the orcish mage read between 4,581 and 6,474
across 30 boots), and every fight is against another fresh roll.

### The company fight

From build 246, `BotRoomTrial` (`BotRoomTrial.cs`) puts up to five of a guild's fighters, doubled, against a
dungeon's worst room: the spawner whose creatures add up to most, up to six creatures. In these logs the rooms were
Wrong (4 golem controllers) and Despise (5 ogre lords). A guild may delve past the easiest dungeon only if its company
cleared that dungeon's room within the last two hours.

### From logs to data

`extract.py` reads the lines the code writes: one line per fight (`BotTrial.Say`), one per company fight
(`BotRoomTrial.Say`), one per second of a traced bow fight (`BotTrial.Trace`), the five-minute summary
(`BotProving.Describe`), the ladder at boot, the delve's lines, bot deaths, and the door's answers to `awake`. The
fight line changed twice: the part "the double held X, N weapon changes" exists from build 238, and "the double took
N steps, fired N and stood paralysed N s" from build 252. All 1,248 fight lines parse. The shard's own five-minute
counters (fights held, won, lost, called, companies, cleared) were compared with the parsed lines at all 149 summary
lines, and they agree every time.

The logs do not print a build number. `extract.py` maps each session to a build (`data/sessions.csv`, column
`basis`). Most are fixed by a marker in the log itself (the only R 20 readings, the first line of a new kind, the
wake's boot line); builds 237, 240, 242, 243, 244, 258, 261 and 263 are placed by deploy order only. The boundary
that matters most is exact: the boot of build 249 at 03:43 warns that everything measured before was measured
against sleeping creatures.

Three periods are used below:

## 3. What was found

### 3.1 For two hours the creatures on the ring were asleep

The engine runs a creature's AI only in map sectors a player's client has woken. `Map.Sector.OnEnter` activates the
surrounding sectors only for a mobile with a network connection, and `AITimer.ShouldStop` switches off the AI of a
creature whose sector is inactive. A sleeping creature does not walk, cast or flee. It still swings back at whoever
stands next to it, because the swing is the engine's combat timer, not the AI. Green Acres never has a client, and
bots have no connection.

The fights of builds 234-247 show it:

- 51 of the 214 fights ended with the double untouched. 47 of those 51 doubles held a bow, standing six tiles off.
- Of 66 fights that began with a bow in hand, 32 were called (nobody died in 180 s, or nobody was hurt for 45 s). Of
  129 fights with a blade, staff or fists, 5 were called.
- 60 fights read R at its cap. In build 247 Brannoc the archer's double stood 180 s against a lich lord, took 0
  damage, and read R 5, which is 140,239 of strength against his formula value of 1,741.
- The very first readings were walkovers. In build 234, Doran the Captain beat the orcish mage in 25 s taking 0
  damage and read R 20, which is 112,185 of strength (formula 2,250). He beat the juka mage in 106 s, again taking 0.

From build 249 (03:43) the rings' sectors are woken before every fight, and every earlier reading was thrown away.
The same fights then went the other way: the win rate fell from 39% (83 of 214) to 14% (103 of 747); against the orcish mage
from 48% (49 of 102) to 15% (98 of 643). Only 2 of the 747 awake fights left the double untouched. Company fights
cleared their room in 5 of 14 while asleep, and in 2 of 101 awake.

### 3.2 The whole world sleeps where no player is

The same rule holds everywhere a bot goes: with nobody logged in, the creatures round the bots have their AI off. The
door verb `awake` (build 251) counts, round every bot, the creatures whose AI timer runs:

| Time | Wake | Creatures within 18 tiles of a bot | AI on | Inside dungeons, AI on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 04:07:51 | off | 47 | 0 | 0 of 18 |
| 04:08:31 | on inside dungeons (dialled 8 s before) | 46 | 22 | 22 of 22 |
| 04:14:29 | off | 16 | 0 | 0 of 0 |
| 13:17:08 | on everywhere (build 259) | 128 | 128 | 16 of 16 |

There were 0 clients on the shard at every reading.

For 5.97 minutes (04:08:23 to 04:14:21) the dungeons round the bots were woken by a dial. There were nine bot deaths
in the Orc Caves in that time, 90.5 an hour. They were seven bots; Elspeth died three times, raised into the same room
by her party. The killers were an orc bomber four times, an orc, an orcish lord, an orcish mage, and two unknown.
Earlier the same night, from 01:08 to 04:00, with the world asleep, there were 45 bot deaths on the dungeon side of the
map (x ≥ 5120), 15.7 an hour. The wake was switched off after six minutes. Build 259 (13:16) switched it on for good,
together with a rule that a bot must carry its class's supplies before a delve or a war; the decision log gives these
nine deaths as the reason the two were shipped together.

### 3.3 The formula is right on average and wrong for each bot

On average the formula's scale is not far off. Per fight, proven strength divided by `Power` had a median of 1.10
with the rings awake (builds 249-258, 747 fights). The shard computes the same thing every five minutes (the median
of proven strength over `Power` for readings with R under 3, `BotProving.Calibration`); with the rings awake it stayed
between 1.04 and 1.32 (10th to 90th percentile of 134 summaries).

For the individual bot it is wrong. The middle 80% of those 747 fights lie between 0.18 and 5.9 times the formula.
And it ranks the bots the wrong way. Against the orcish mage, rings awake, for the 47 bots with at least three such
fights, the rank correlation (Spearman) between each bot's median R and its median value of:

| Measure | Correlation with R |
|---|---|
| `Power` (the formula) | -0.47 |
| HitsMax alone | +0.77 |
| Sum of the fighting skills | +0.67 |
| Str + Dex + Int | +0.67 |
| Armour rating | +0.46 |
| Weapon's average hit | -0.10 |

Over the 868 fights themselves (not independent, the same bots fought many times) the signs are the same: `Power`
-0.12, HitsMax +0.49, fighting skills +0.46. Hit points, one factor of the formula, rank the bots well. The other
factor, average hit plus Magery / 2, turns the ranking over: it raises casters, and casters lose.

By class, rings awake (builds 249-264):

| Class | Fights | Won | Win rate | Median `Power` | Median HitsMax | Median fighting skills |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Warrior | 331 | 116 | 35% | 1,529 | 80 | 284 |
| Archer | 250 | 17 | 7% | 1,748 | 68 | 220 |
| WarriorMage | 126 | 10 | 8% | 3,176 | 74 | 271.5 |
| WarriorArcher | 124 | 9 | 7% | 1,856 | 71 | 228.5 |
| Mage | 94 | 0 | 0% | 2,787.5 | 64 | 167.5 |
| Healer | 48 | 0 | 0% | 1,912 | 63 | 160 |
| Brawler | 20 | 1 | 5% | 1,710 | 87 | 225.5 |
| Captain | 20 | 7 | 35% | 2,282 | 90 | 398.5 |
| Baron | 15 | 6 | 40% | 2,646 | 98 | 496 |
| Sage | 6 | 0 | 0% | 3,591 | 63 | 286 |

The formula also says no bot can beat an orcish mage alone: `Power` / might was at most 0.62 (median 0.30) in all
868 awake fights against it. 157 of them were won.

The proving README and the decision log (build 248) say the formula was off by 0.07 to 13 in the first hour. That
range is not reproduced by the fight lines: the 95 fights of the first hour give 0 to 231, and they were fought
against sleeping creatures.

### 3.4 Fresh bots lose to awake creatures

The world had been wiped the evening before (build 233, fifty novice bots). With the rings awake:

- Builds 249-258: 103 of 747 fights won. Median R 0.30. A lost fight took a median of 27 s (10th to 90th percentile
  16 to 42 s).
- The first 35 awake fights (03:43 to 03:51) lost 30. The decision log's "33 of 35 lost" counts build 250's boot
  only (the shard's counters restart at every boot): its first 35 fights, 03:45 to 03:53, lost 33.
- Above rung 1, 166 fights were held and 9 won, by two bots. Edda the Baron (495-496 in the fighting skills, armour
  50) beat the ogre lord twice, the lich lord and the dragon. Dain the Warrior (296-324 in the fighting skills) beat
  the juka mage five times. Every other bot lost every fight above the orcish mage.
- Company fights: 99 of 101 wiped out, in a median of 27 s, having taken a median of 14% of the room's health. Two
  cleared Wrong's four golem controllers: The Blade at 12:57:36 (40 s, 3 of 5 fell) and The Lantern at 14:51:31
  (25 s, 1 of 5 fell).
- Casters did worst. Mages won 0 of 94 fights and cast a spell in 16 of them. Healers won 0 of 48, the Sage 0 of 6.
  The decision log (build 247) found the mages' packs without the reagents their attack spells need.

After 13:16 (build 259) the win rate against the orcish mage rose from 15% (98 of 643) to 26% (59 of 225). The median
fighting skills of those who fought it also rose, from 224 to 254. The doubles used fewer bandages per fight than
before (0.61 against 0.85), so the new supply rule does not show in these fights. What caused the rise is not
established.

### 3.5 Archers: weak, not broken

Archers lost to the orcish mage having dealt nearly nothing: at 04:09:19 Brannoc's double (150 arrows) lost in 51 s
having dealt 0, at 04:10:55 Hollis's (130 arrows) in 27 s having dealt 0. Three explanations were checked.

- Paralysis. The orcish mage casts Paralyze. The engine's `Mobile.Damage` sets `Paralyzed = false`, so any hit breaks
  it. Build 252 made the fight line report the seconds the double stood paralysed. In the 934 fights from build 252
  on, 224 (24%) had at least one second of it; when it happened the median was 3 s, the 90th percentile 6 s, the
  longest 27 s. Between 04:20 and 04:28, when this was first read, it was 0 to 9 s per fight. Not the main cause.
- The shot counter. In builds 252-253 the "fired N" count included the arrows that fall to the corpse at death
  (Cassia "fired 13" in 28 s having dealt 0). Build 254 counts shots only while the double stands.
- The bow itself. Build 254 traced the first three bow fights after each boot once a second (29 traced fights up to
  15:30). The creature was within one tile by the 2nd to 6th second (median 4th). Shots came every 5 s (median;
  10th to 90th percentile 4 to 6 s at one-second resolution), and the swing timer read 4.0 to 5.7 s just after a
  shot (10th to 90th percentile). The double fired at the rate the era allows.

One real defect was found: in build 254's three traced fights the double's first arrow flew only at 7 to 8 s (the
swing timer read 7,299 to 7,301 ms at the start), because the bow was equipped before the double's stamina was
filled. Build 255 sets the double's swing timer to "now"; after it, the first arrow flew at 0 s in all 22 traced
fights that began with arrows. The decision log's conclusion (build 255) is that the archers' doubles fight
correctly and that archers are weak against an awake orcish mage under the era's rules, not broken. Of the 295 fights
(builds 252-264) that began with a bow in hand, 32 were won. With the count fixed (285 fights from build 254), a bow
fight saw a median of 4 shots, and 56 saw none.

### 3.6 How the readings were used, and the first mistakes

The delve judged bands by proven strength from the first minute. Build 234 capped R at 20 and scaled a walkover up the
ladder. Within five seconds (01:36:51 to 01:36:56) the delve logged eight moved offers (it logs at most eight per
boot): five to Terathan Keep and three to Shame, with bands worth 89,881 to 429,827 by the proving ground against
2,167 to 9,274 by the formula. The shard's calibration factor read ×37.71 at 01:40:50. The decision log records that
none of these offers was taken and that judging was switched off by dial at 01:36:58. The delve's own counter of offers sent deeper than the formula would
have sent them read 9 in build 234's session, 101 in build 240's, 128 in build 246's, and 0 in every session from
build 250 to 258.

## 4. What changed in the code because of it

From the decision log, builds 234-259:

| Build | Change |
|---|---|
| 234 | The proving ground: doubles, rings, R, the ladder; the delve judges bands by proven strength. |
| 235 | R capped at 5 (was 20); nothing is extrapolated up the ladder (a bot measured only lower is worth at most min(R, 1) × the might of the strongest creature it beat × 0.75); the calibration uses only readings with R under 3, needs five, and is clamped to 0.25-4. |
| 236 | A band is reckoned in the order the call raises it (guildmates first, then nearest), not as its strongest four. |
| 237 | The party actually raised is reckoned again before it goes down. |
| 238 | The double copies the whole pack and draws its blade as the bot does; the line says what it held. |
| 242 | Every member of a band, the leader included, must be worth at least 0.2 of the dungeon's worst creature (not for the easiest dungeon). |
| 245 | A reading goes stale on max(5 points, 6% of the fighting skills), not on 5 points. |
| 246 | Company fights against a dungeon's worst room; past the easiest dungeon only with a room cleared within two hours. |
| 247 | Room readings survive a restart; a shooter's double brings its bow up. |
| 248 | The dashboard shows "beats" (the deepest dungeon whose worst creature the bot has beaten) instead of `Power`. |
| 249 | The rings are woken before every fight; every earlier reading is dropped (store shape 3). |
| 250 | The easiest dungeon is judged by the old formula again: an awake orcish mage beats nearly every fresh bot one to one, while bands still came back from the Orc Caves. |
| 251 | `BotWake` (off by default) and the door verb `awake`; a bot's build is the arms it carries. |
| 252 | The fight line reports steps, shots and seconds paralysed. |
| 253 | A bot's build is its best blade and its best bow. |
| 254 | Bow fights are traced once a second; shots are counted only while the double stands. |
| 255 | The double's first swing is no longer late; the quiver is no longer part of the build. |
| 259 | The world is woken round every bot (`Wake: true`, `WakeDeepsOnly: false`); a bot below its class's supply threshold is not taken on a delve or into another guild's ground in a war. |

## 5. Open questions

- The formula still decides wherever no fight has been measured: the easiest dungeon (build 250), bots never
  measured (formula × calibration), and, per the decision log, a guild's might in war declarations. No replacement
  has been built. In these data hit points alone ranked bots better than the formula, but that was measured against
  one creature, the orcish mage.
- The readings are noisy. Might comes from one random creature per boot, each fight meets another, a reading averages
  at most four fights, and a bot is re-measured when its build drifts. How much of R's spread is noise was not
  measured.
- The double never flees and never chooses its fight. For a lost fight R is about the share of the creature killed;
  what the real bot would have done is not measured.
- Almost everything was measured on rung 1: 868 of the 1,034 awake fights were against the orcish mage. Above it only
  two bots ever won.
- Mages: 0 wins in 94 fights, a spell in 16. Whether the supply threshold of build 259 changes this is not yet
  visible.
- Why the win rate against the orcish mage rose after 13:16 is open (see 3.4).
- A single fight is not a room. Single-fight readings still count in the band's sum, while companies cleared 2 rooms
  in 101 with the creatures awake.
- The 0.07-13 range quoted for the first hour could not be reproduced from the logs (see 3.3).

## 6. How to reproduce

    python extract.py path/to/logs --until "2026-09-26 15:30:00"

The logs folder must hold the shard's `session-YYYY-MM-DD_HH-MM.log` files. If it also holds
`bot-debugger-commands.log` (the door's log), the `awake` answers go to `data/census.csv`. The script rewrites
`data/` and the data block inside `index.html`. Without `--until` it reads everything, and the numbers of the last
period grow while the shard runs; the numbers in this write-up are those of the command above. It takes about 20
seconds on 400 MB of logs.

On the shard, through the door (the shard's command channel to its debugger bot, Argus):

- `do prove <bot> [<Creature>]`: that bot's double fights next, against the named creature or its due rung.
- `do proof <bot>`: every reading of one bot, and its strength against every dungeon's worst.
- `do proofs`: every bot measured, strongest first, and the ladder.
- `do awake`: of the creatures near the bots, how many have their AI on.
- Dials: `BotProving.Judges false` (the delve back on the formula), `BotProving.Running false` (no fights),
  `BotProving.TraceRanged <n>` (trace the next n bow fights), `BotWake.Running`, `BotWake.DeepsOnly`.
- Settings: `Configuration/bot-proving.json`, keys `Enabled`, `Judges`, `Rings`, `CapMs`, `StillMs`, `PauseMs`,
  `RetestMs`, `Pass`, `Reach`, `Keep`, `Wake`, `WakeDeepsOnly` (PascalCase; a lower-case key is silently ignored).
