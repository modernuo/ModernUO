Work weights

How a bot on this shard decides what a piece of work is worth, and so what it does next — read from the code and measured on every decision the bots logged. Each chart below is drawn from the numbers extract.py writes to data/; the method is explained in README.md.

The weighing, step by step

A proposer offers a deed with a claim in gold-equivalent per minute. The claim is corrected by what that kind of work has paid across the island, then by what this bot has earned at this place, bent by the fifth root of the factors, multiplied by who the bot is and whose land it is, and compared. Gates can end the weighing at nought at three points; the counts on them are the first refusals printed on take lines.

What the claim became by the time it won

For the twenty kinds taken most often: the median estimate on the take line (the claim after the island's correction and the bot's own ledger) against the median rate it won at (after the factors and the multipliers). Rates above the estimate come from the bot's own trade (× 1.30) or a boost (a revel, its guild's land or charter); below it, from the factors. Log scale; hover a row for the quartiles.

Every refused offer, where the shard keeps a counter

The take line names only the first refused offer of a review. The five-minute census counts every refusal for the gates that keep a counter; these are the last census of each session, summed.

Claims that live on the floor

Inside the "expected to pay nothing" refusals the line prints both numbers: the claim as corrected by the island, and what this bot expects here once its own ledger (or the island's record of the patch) is folded in. In this window the median claims of prowl, unload, band and drill sat at a quarter of their typed claims (8, 30, 30 and 35) — the floor under the island's correction — while the ledger had them below zero.
