# Pathfinding: why the bots got stuck, and the tiered navigation that replaced one bounded search

Part of the research notes of an open-source project: autonomous bots on a ModernUO shard (Ultima Online, Renaissance
era). This topic covers how a bot finds its way, why that failed for a month, and what replaced it on 26.09.2026.

- Numbers from the shard's session logs are regenerated by [`extract.py`](extract.py) into [`data/`](data/). The
  quoted ones are in [`data/key_numbers.json`](data/key_numbers.json).
- Numbers from the project's decision log (`Projects/BotAIv2/DECISIONS.md`) or from code comments are marked
  *(decision log)* or *(code)*. They were measured at the time and are not re-derived here.
- Charts: [`index.html`](index.html) (one self-contained file; open it in a browser).
- "Measured on 26.09.2026" is the default date. Times are the shard's local time.

## In short

- A bot's walk was **one A\* search over tiles, from where the bot stood, stopped by a clock**: 4 to 60 ms for an
  ordinary plan, 150 ms for the last look for a way round. Such a search cannot see a way round that is longer than
  what it can examine before the clock stops it. West of Britain a river puts ground 84 tiles apart about 1,400
  steps apart by road.
- For a month the fixes changed **the clock, the notes bots leave about places, and the rules for refusing work**.
  None changed the method. The design of 20.08.2026 had ruled out a node graph on purpose.
- On 26.09.2026 the method was replaced: a **coarse chart** over the road map (builds 262 and 263), then a
  **navigation graph of the whole map in the engine** (build 264). Builds 265 to 268 followed within the hour: a
  first cut of the **long tier**, routes that end within reach of the goal, and the graph **kept on disk** between
  boots. This write-up stops at build 268.
- Same day, same population, same age of session. In its first 45 minutes the old walker spent **562 s** of the
  game loop on searches (**20.8 %** of the time) and **38.4 %** of its searches ended partial. Build 263, in its
  first 45 minutes: **40 s (1.5 %)** and **3.7 %**. Build 264, in its first 10 minutes: **15 s (2.5 %)** and **4.1 %**.
- The graph of Felucca: **114,688 clusters** of 16×16 tiles, **285,550 nodes**, **172,994 gates**, **2,478,736
  directed edges**, **1,216 connected components**, drawn on the game loop in **17.6 s** of loop time spread over
  **100 s**. The way from the east bank to The Lantern's hall door: 105 points, **1,381 steps** for 84 straight,
  29,819 nodes expanded in **7.6 ms**.

## 1. How a bot walked until 26.09.2026

The walker lives in `Projects/BotAIv2/BotMovement`. The pieces that matter here:

- **`BotPath`**: an A\* over tiles, using the engine's own step masks (`StepCache`, `StepProbe`), so plan and engine
  answer "can I step there" by the same rules. A step costs 10 straight and 14 diagonal. Heights are folded into
  bands of 20 units (`BotStep.Cell`), as in the engine's own A\*.
- **A ceiling in time, not in nodes.** A search looks at the clock every 64 expansions. An ordinary plan gets
  0.25 ms per tile of distance, between 4 and 60 ms (`MsPerTile`, `ShortMs`, `CeilingMs`). A plan for a walk that has
  stopped closing gets the whole 60 ms. A formation station gets 15 ms. The whole population may spend 500 ms a
  second (`WindowMs`); past that a search gets 1 ms (`FloorMs`).
- **A search box**: 256 tiles either side of the straight line between the bot and its goal (`MinMargin`,
  `MaxMargin`).
- **Three answers**: `Reached`; `Partial` (walk to the tile nearest the goal and ask again from there); `Sealed`
  (the ground ran out inside the box: provably no way, filed for everybody in `BotReach`).
- **The escalation for a walk that is not closing** (`BotWalk.Plan`): the whole ceiling; after two plans that got no
  nearer, a look at the far side (`BotPath.Enclose`, up to 2,500 cells in 30 ms, to prove the goal sits in a closed
  pocket); if the far side is "the world", one search at the stranded ceiling of 150 ms; if that ends partial and no
  nearer, the errand is dropped: **"no way round it was found in 150ms"**. Eight empty plans or twelve plans without
  getting nearer end it as **"could not get one tile closer ... in N plans"**.

### Why that cannot see round a river

A\* with a straight-line estimate looks first at the tiles that point at the goal. A way round a river starts by
walking away from the goal. Before the search finds it, it must look at the ground the way round passes through,
and the clock and the box stop it first. It then answers `Partial` with a plan to the tile nearest the goal: the
river bank. The bot walks to the bank and asks again from there, with the same result.

The summary line shows the signature. In the session 13:16–14:05, 27,739 of 72,294 searches ended partial and
**11,141 of those ended no nearer than they started**. 13,324 asked for the whole 60 ms because the walk had stopped
closing; the largest single spender of the whole ceiling was "to the counter of The Lantern", 3,479 times. 49
searches ran at the 150 ms ceiling; **2 found a way round**. At the measured rate of that session (0.35 µs a tile:
1,629,443,823 tiles in 561,778 ms) a 150 ms search examines on the order of 430,000 tiles. The way round is long
in the other direction too: the graph's route from the east bank to The Lantern's door (section 5.4) is 84 tiles
from the goal at its start and 244 tiles from it at its twelfth point, (1328, 1247).

The concrete case of 26.09.2026 *(decision log, builds 256–262)*. The Lantern's hall was moved to 1082,1402, across
the river from where its members work. Its company was called to the hall's door at (1084, 1406) three times
between 14:05 and 14:14 and failed each time, "no way round it was found in 150ms". The road map put the door
1,439 steps from home by road for 356 straight. The east bank at (1168, 1384) is 84 tiles from the door in a
straight line.

## 3. The road map from home: `BotRoads` (build 63, 15.09.2026)

The first piece of knowledge about the road rather than the straight line.

- **What it is.** One breadth-first flood from the population's home at (1440, 1470, 0), stepping exactly as the
  planner steps (the engine's step masks, both flanks of a diagonal clear, no item where the step lands), inside a
  square of 640 tiles each way. Each tile keeps the steps of the shortest road to it from home.
- **What it costs.** It runs on the game loop in slices of at most 8 ms every 100 ms. Measured at 147 boots in the
  logs: **959,661 to 959,844 tiles** reached, the farthest **1,781 steps** of road, **1.7 to 3.5 s** of loop time
  over **16 to 31 s**.
- **What it can say.** Only distances from home. From anywhere else it gives a lower bound: the road from A to B is
  at least road(home, B) − road(home, A). `Detour`, `Behind` and `Farther` turn that into "how far round" a place
  lies. The hunt's darts, lairs, seams, fires, forges and counters, the harrow's levy and (from build 263) the price
  of work across the river all read it.
- **What it cannot do.** Give a route. It knows how far, not which way.

## 4. The coarse chart: `BotChart` (builds 262 and 263)

Build 262 drew a two-level map over the road map and asked it through the console (the "door"), without walking it.
Build 263 put the walker on it.

- **Squares of 16 tiles** over the road map's square of 1,281 × 1,281 tiles: 81 × 81 = 6,561 squares.
- **Gates.** Wherever a body can step across the edge between two squares, each run of such steps is a gate, with a
  node on each side; a run longer than 8 gets two gates, one near each end.
- **Ways inside a square.** A small flood from each node gives the steps to the square's other nodes.
- **A route** is an A\* over the nodes with the Chebyshev distance as the estimate, returning one point a square.
  Each point is a short leg the tile search finds at once. A gate a leg could not pass is shunned for 10 minutes.
- **No look at the world of its own.** It reads the steps `BotRoads` recorded, kept per tile at the height the tile
  was first reached. So a bridge and the road under it share a tile, and the chart covers only the road map's square.

Measured at boot: **44,696 nodes and 22,380 gates on 6,561 squares, 490,500 ways**, drawn in **234 ms** of the loop
over **3.0 s** on build 262 and 274 ms over 3.5 s on build 263. Over the seven boots that drew it by the time the logs
were read: 44,694–44,696 nodes, 234–317 ms of the loop over 3.0–4.1 s.

Routes asked through the door on build 262 (from `bot-debugger-commands.log`):

|---|---|---|---|---|
| east bank (1168, 1384) → The Lantern's door (1084, 1406) | 84 | 123 points, about 1,457 steps | 31,102 | 10.09 ms |
| home (1440, 1470) → The Lantern's door | 356 | 130 points, about 1,452 steps | 33,620 | 5.62 ms |

**How build 263 walked it.** An errand whose target is more than 24 tiles away (`DirectTiles`) is walked over the
route: each plan goes to the furthest of the next few points within 24 tiles of road (`LegTiles`), arriving within
3 (`LegReached`). The route is drawn again when the goal moves by half a leg or the bot strays 24 tiles; after 4
redraws the errand is walked straight, as before. Progress is measured along the route, so walking round the river
no longer reads as walking away. Work across a river is priced by the road (`BotRoads.Farther`).

**What it did, first 10 minutes** (14:32–14:42): 12,805 searches for 13.5 s of the loop (1.33 s a minute; the old
walker at the same age: 11.3 s), **870 partial (6.8 %)**, 481 no nearer, **5,412 plans to a leg of a chart route,
none of which failed**. Over its whole session of 46 minutes: 55,722 searches for 40.0 s, 3.7 % partial, 22,915 leg
plans and 0 failed legs.

**Where it was weak** *(decision log, build 264)*: the route lived in one slot of the journey, so a fight pushed on
top threw it away; a failed leg switched the progress measures back to the straight line mid-walk; a company's
stations, re-issued every 1.5 s, each drew a route; a far bank twenty tiles across a river was never routed; three
errands' own watches still measured the straight line.

## 5. Tiered navigation in the engine (build 264)

On 26.09.2026 the owner asked for "the implementation of the different levels of path search" *(decision log)* and
supplied a design document. It is summarised here, then compared with what was built.

### 5.1 The design: Short, Medium, Long

The design is a funnel from coarse to fine: plan coarse, refine lazily, execute locally.

| tier | ground it sees | question it answers | answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Long** | regions of 128×128 tiles (8×8 clusters), portals (teleporters, moongates) | which regions and portals | a corridor of regions |
| **Medium** | clusters of 16×16 tiles, gates between them (hierarchical A\*, HPA\*) | through which gaps | waypoints, about one per cluster |
| **Short** | tiles, with everything dynamic: creatures, doors, items | where to put the next foot | the steps to the next waypoint |

Its rules, in short:

- The upper tiers see only static ground, with houses and boats as an overlay. Everything that moves is the Short
  tier's business.
- The upper tiers never lie about reachability. "No way" means no way on the static ground; "a way" means the Short
  tier can walk every leg of it in an empty world.
- One cost model on every tier, or the tiers disagree and the path jitters between them.
- The tier is chosen by distance and topology, not by who asks: Short for up to 32 tiles in one component; Medium
  for up to 512 tiles with no portal on the way; Long beyond that, across maps or into dungeons. Two ends in
  different components with no portal between are unreachable at once, with no search.
- Connected components by union-find give "unreachable" in one comparison. The document calls this the main CPU
  saving for bots: hopeless goals are cut before any search.
- Not to do: jump point search (the ground has soft costs, several floors per cell and moving creatures), a navmesh
  (the world is a grid of 8-direction steps), any-angle planners, D\* Lite for chases, the engine's step cache from a
  background thread (it is not thread-safe), storing every cluster edge's tile path.
- Acceptance targets: Medium within 5 % of an oracle on average (95th percentile within 12 %) on 5,000 random pairs
  of 32–512 tiles, no false "unreachable", 1 ms per route at the 95th percentile. Long: a bot from Britain reaches a
  dungeon through the teleporters without hand-set waypoints; a moongate is chosen when shorter than walking; 2 ms at
  the 95th percentile; half of all long routes answered from a route cache.

It also listed five faults of the engine's stock A\* (which the bots never used): a squared-distance estimate that
makes the search greedy, heights folded into 20-unit planes so two floors 16 units apart collide, octile costs that
do not match the game's step time, a path follower that drops height, and a 38-tile window.

### 5.2 One cost model

- A step costs **100**, straight or diagonal: the engine's step delay does not depend on direction (walk 400 ms,
  run 200 ms, mounted 200 and 100 ms, per the design document). A diagonal costs **1 more**, only to break ties
  towards straighter paths.
- The estimate is **100 × Chebyshev distance** (the larger of |dx| and |dy|). Every step costs at least 100, so it
  never overestimates: A\* stays optimal on the graph it searches.
- All costs are integers. A door is priced at 200 in `NavCost`, but the graph does not use it yet (see 6.5).
- For the Long tier with portals the document rules out a geometric estimate (a teleporter crosses half the map for
  about nothing) and proposes Dijkstra or landmarks (ALT).

### 5.3 What was built

In `Projects/UOContent/Engines/Pathing/Tiered`:

| file | what it does |
|---|---|
| `NavCost` | the cost model above |
| `NavTerrain` | the ground: the engine's static step probe, and its house-aware form in any 16-tile sector a house touches (and the ring round it) |
| `NavWindow` | one cluster plus a one-tile ring, probed lazily. Every standable surface of a cell is its own **stratum** (up to 4). A step lands on the neighbour's surface within 3 units of where the engine says it lands. A diagonal needs both flanking steps open. The graph, the planner and the tests all step through this one function |
| `NavGraph` | the first level. **Clusters of 16×16** (the size of a map sector and of a step-cache chunk). **Entrances per pair of strata** across each cluster edge, so a bridge deck and the road under it are separate entrances: a run of up to 6 crossings gets one gate in its middle, a longer run two gates at its ends. **Directed edges**: a drop the engine allows is not always a climb it allows back. Edges inside a cluster come from a flood from each node. **Drawn a cluster at a time, outward from home**, 8 ms every 50 ms on the game loop. **Union-find components** once complete (edges taken as undirected, which can only call too much connected, never too little). A cluster whose ground changes is torn down with its four edges and drawn again, node ids kept stable |
| `NavWindowCache` | the 64 most-used probed clusters, since bots start and end at the same few places |
| `HierarchicalPlanner` | the Medium tier. Joins the start to its cluster's nodes with a forward flood and the goal with a backward flood; answers `Direct` when both are joined inside one cluster, `Unreachable` from the components without searching, otherwise A\* over the nodes, ties broken towards the node nearer the goal; returns the far node of each gate, one point a cluster. A gate a walker could not pass is shunned for 10 minutes at the cost of 40 steps |
| `NavigationService` | one graph per map; `Prepare`, `Route`, `GroundChanged`, `Shun`; settings `pathfinding.tiered.*` and `pathfinding.medium.*` |

`BaseHouse` tells the navigation when a house is raised, moved or taken down (`GroundChanged`), so the clusters
under it are drawn again ahead of anything else.

**How the bots use it** (`BotJourney.Leg`). The graph is asked first and the old chart only where the graph cannot
answer yet. A far target is routed; so is a near one once its straight plan stops closing, if the road map says one
end lies 40 or more steps further round than the other (a river between them, `RoundTiles`); a company's stations
and sweeps never are. The route belongs to
its errand, so a fight pushed on top keeps its own and the road survives underneath. Every progress measure restarts
when a route is drawn. Each leg is still walked by `BotPath`, the tile search: that is the Short tier.

**Where it departs from the design.**

- The graph is drawn on the game loop in 8 ms slices, not by a thread pool. The project's rule is that work needing
  game state is chunked across ticks on the loop, and the engine's step cache is not thread-safe.
- No graph file: build 264 drew the graph again at every boot (build 267 added one, 6.3).
- No capability sets: one graph, for walkers. No swimmers, no door-openers as a separate class.
- The Medium tier's budget was 60,000 nodes on build 264 (the document: 20,000); see 6.1 for what became of it.
  `Direct` only when both ends are joined inside one cluster (the document: one cluster or two neighbouring ones).
- The Short tier is still `BotPath`, with its 10/14 step costs and 20-unit height bands. The shared cost model and
  strata stop at the Medium tier.

### 5.5 The tests

`Projects/UOContent.Tests/Tests/Engines/Pathing/Tiered`, on a synthetic grid (`GridNavTerrain`) whose cells can hold
ground, walls, water, bridge decks over water or over a road, high ground and ramps. A step climbs at most 5 and
drops at most 10. The oracle is a Dijkstra over every tile and stratum. Nine tests with build 264, all passing
*(decision log; not re-run for this write-up)*:

1. an open field: the route within 10 % of the oracle, and walkable leg by leg;
2. two ends joined inside one cluster: `Direct`, at the oracle's cost;
3. a wall with a gap: the route goes through the gap;
4. a sealed wall: `Unreachable` with **no node expanded** (the components answer);
5. a bridge deck over a road: the road passes under, the deck over, and neither reaches the other;
6. a one-way drop: walked down, not up;
7. a graph not yet drawn: `Pending`;
8. a house raised and taken down: the graph follows, and ends with the same nodes and edges it began with;
9. 300 random pairs among random walls: **295 routes, 5.4 % over the oracle on average, 18.6 % at worst, never
   cheaper, never a false "unreachable"**.

Builds 265 to 267 added four: a river with one far ford and a sealed river (the Long tier), a goal walled in on all
eight sides that is reached with a reach of two, and a graph written to disk and read back that plans the same
routes at the same cost. Thirteen pass *(decision log)*. The synthetic tests never produced a false "unreachable";
the live world did in its first 10 minutes, for a reason the tests did not model (arrival within a reach).

## 6. What came next, and what is still missing

Four more builds went up within the hour after build 264. Each session was short, so these are first readings.

### 6.1 Build 265: a first cut of the long tier (deployed 15:30)

*(decision log)* `StrategicPlanner`: regions of 8×8 clusters (128 tiles). A region's border nodes are the first-level
nodes on its outer edge; its edges come from a Dijkstra from each border node over the region's first-level edges;
a region is rebuilt when any of its clusters has changed. An A\* over border nodes gives the regions a route passes
through; those regions and the ring round them are the corridor the Medium tier then searches alone. The code asks
the Medium tier first, with a budget of 12,000 nodes, and hands a route over budget to the Long tier. The graph's
"no way" is logged and the walker plans straight instead.

In the logs: **1,792 regions of 128 tiles** on Felucca (the design document's 56 × 32). In the synthetic river test
the Long tier's route costs 0.9 % over the oracle with 916 region nodes and 3,092 corridor nodes, against 4,291 for
the Medium tier alone *(decision log)*.

**The Long tier was never asked on the live shard.** Every five-minute summary since says "the long tier took 0
routes over budget", and the console's five benchmarks (6.3) handed it 0 of 1,400 routes. The shard's configuration
file had kept `pathfinding.medium.maxExpansions` at 60,000 from build 264's first boot, and a stored setting wins over
the code's default (`ServerConfiguration.GetOrUpdateSetting`). So the Medium tier keeps searching to 60,000 nodes;
the worst Medium route in the benchmarks took 16.9 ms.

Its session lasted 6 minutes. In its first 5 minutes the walker spent 27.9 s on searches (5.5 s a minute) and 29.8 %
of 4,170 searches ended partial, far worse than build 264 at the same age. The summary names the largest spender of
the whole ceiling: 299 searches "to the muster at (1084, 1406)", The Lantern's door again. The graph said "no way" on
214 plans, for 3 bot-and-target pairs; **213 of them came before the graph had finished drawing**. The Medium planner
answers `Unreachable` when its open list empties, and on a graph still being drawn that only means "not within what
is drawn so far". Not analysed further here.

### 6.2 Build 266: a route to within reach (deployed 15:37)

*(decision log)* The route now ends anywhere within the errand's arrival distance of the goal, as the tile search
does: the goal's own cell first, then ring by ring out to the reach, and the first cell joined to the start by the
components is the goal. Its session ended after 5 minutes, before its first summary: nothing to measure.

### 6.3 Build 267: the graph kept on disk, and a benchmark on the live map (deployed 15:42)

*(decision log)* Once the graph is whole, a copy of its arrays is taken on the loop and a worker thread writes it to
`Data/Pathfinding/navgraph-<map>.nvg`, never while the world saves. At the next boot a worker thread reads it back if
it was drawn from the same map files (the step cache's fingerprint), and the loop puts it in place; clusters by a
house, then or now, are drawn again. In the logs: **written to disk in 151 ms** (36 MB, *decision log*).

The console gained `navbench n radius checked`: `n` random pairs of standable tiles within `radius` of home (the
same pairs every time: the random generator is seeded), routed over the graph. For the first `checked` routes found,
every leg is planned by the tile search, and the route's length is set against the tile search's own plan from end
to end at three times its ceiling, where that plan reaches the goal: the nearest thing to an oracle the live map has.

### 6.4 Build 268: the first boot from disk (deployed 15:55)

The log says "the graph of Felucca was read from disk", one second after the boot began drawing it: **234 ms of the
loop** to put it in place, then the clusters by houses (the log counts 135) drawn again for another 970 ms of the
loop. A full drawing had cost 17.6 to 21.2 s of the loop on the four boots before it. The written graph was made
off the loop by a worker thread (151 ms).

*(decision log)* The same build makes the Medium tier's search lean towards the goal: a step costs the same straight
or diagonal, so the Chebyshev estimate leaves wide plateaus of equal cost, and ties are now broken by one part in
1,000 of the estimate. On the same seeded benchmark the Medium tier expanded 1,959,599 nodes in each of three runs,
against 1,969,656 on build 267 (0.5 % fewer), and took 2.66 to 3.11 ms a route on average against 2.07. Node counts
are the steadier measure; the milliseconds depend on what else the loop is doing.

### 6.5 Still missing

- **The Long tier in use** on the live shard (6.1), and **portals**: teleporters and moongates as edges of the Long
  tier (the design document counts 1,368 teleporter entries), with a Dijkstra or landmark (ALT) search that stays
  correct when a step can cross half the map.
- **A route cache** keyed by region pair, since bots walk the same roads (bank, dungeon, vendor).
- **Speed.** On the live map a Medium route between places within 500 tiles of home took 2.1 to 3.1 ms on average
  and 7.0 to 11.4 ms at the 95th percentile (within 1,000 tiles: 4.0 and 11.6 ms), against the design's 1 ms.
- **Items and doors in the graph.** The graph is built from the static step probe (land, statics, houses). This
  shard's world is decorated with items (fences, gravestones), which only the Short tier sees; a gate blocked by an
  item is found by a failed leg and shunned. Doors cost 200 in the model but are not priced in the graph.
- **The Short tier on the shared model**: `BotPath` still costs 10/14 and folds heights into 20-unit bands.
- **Trusting "no way"**: `BotWalk.TrustNoWay` stays off until a window passes with no wrong answer; an answer given
  before the graph is whole should not count as one.
- **The engine's own creatures** on the tiers; they still use the stock A\*.

### 6.6 Open questions

- None of the changes was turned back to see the numbers return (the project's own rule for believing a change).
  The nearest control is build 262, whose 5 minutes ran the old walker beside the drawn chart: 12.1 s of search a
  minute, 33.0 % partial.
- Build 263 changed two things at once: the walker went onto the chart, and work across a river was priced by the
  road, so fewer bots chose it. The logs cannot split the saving between the two.
- On the live map the graph's routes run 4.7 to 9.1 % longer than the tile search's own plans, against 5.4 % over the
  oracle on synthetic grids. Where the difference comes from (the gate placement, the 100/101 costs against the tile
  search's 10/14, or the tile search's own plans) has not been measured.
- 1,216 components on Felucca: how many are real islands, roofs and closed yards, and how many are artefacts of the
  height tolerance or the one-tile ring of a window, has not been counted.
- The worst single search was 312 ms in the old walker's session, 292 ms on build 263 and 188 ms on build 264. The
  tiers make the ceiling rare; they do not remove it.

## 7. Method notes

- **Summaries are cumulative.** "Getting about:" is printed every five minutes with counters since the world loaded.
  A session's row uses its last summary; the age of a summary is its time less the session's first timestamp (the
  bots' clock starts about 4 s after it). Comparisons between sessions use summaries at the same age, as the
  decision log's rule asks.
- **Search time per minute** is the summary's total milliseconds divided by its age. It is time spent in path
  searches on the game loop's thread, so as a share of wall-clock time it is the share of the loop.
- **Session length** runs from the first to the last timestamp in the file, following midnight when a session
  crosses it. The last session may still have been running when the logs were read: its last row is a snapshot.
- **Walk failures** are counted by message shape, only from the walker (`BotWalk`) and the will (`BotWill`). One event
  can write a walker line and a will line, so the columns overlap: "has dropped" includes "no way round it was found"
  and "could not get one tile closer". The drop reasons split every "has dropped" line: the first two are the
  bounded search giving up; the rest are answers proved about the ground. "No way through to" counts only the will's
  failure lines (other summaries contain the phrase). Rates per hour use the session's length; sessions shorter than
  a minute get none.
- **Short sessions are noisy.** A boot re-sends every bot somewhere, so the first minutes of a session carry more
  drops than later ones. The page's "first 10 minutes" view keeps only sessions that lasted 10 minutes or more and
  counts the drops of their first 10 minutes (`e10_*`).
- **Console benchmarks share the counters.** A `navbench` run through the console uses the same tile search and the
  same allowance as the bots, so a session with one is left out of the same-age comparisons (builds 267, 268).
- **Builds** are not named in the logs. A deploy starts a new session file; the build of each marked session comes
  from the decision log.
- **The door's routes and benchmarks** come from `bot-debugger-commands.log`, whose lines have times but no dates.
  Each boot banner there is matched to the session file that began up to five minutes before it.
